For a healthy person with normal bowel movements, a useful rule of thumb is that **food takes about 24–72 hours to travel completely through the digestive tract**. It is not usually true that tonight's or tomorrow morning's bowel movement corresponds neatly to one particular meal.

Typical timing looks roughly like this:

* **Stomach:** about **2–4 hours** for a mixed meal to empty substantially.
* **Small intestine:** another **3–6 hours**. This is where most carbohydrates, protein, fats, vitamins, and minerals are absorbed.
* **Large intestine/colon:** usually **12–48 hours**, sometimes longer. Water is absorbed and the remaining material becomes stool.
* **Total mouth-to-stool transit:** commonly **1–3 days** in healthy adults; around **4 days can still occur without necessarily being abnormal**.

So, for example, if you eat lunch on **Monday**, most of its useful nutrients have probably been absorbed by Monday evening or night. The non-digestible residue may appear in bowel movements sometime from **Tuesday through Thursday**.

One important point is that stool is a **mixture from many meals**. If you have a bowel movement every morning, it does **not** mean yesterday's food has necessarily all left your body. Some material may be from meals eaten two or three days earlier.

Also, "completely extracted" has two meanings. **Nutrient extraction is mostly finished within roughly 6–10 hours**, once material has passed through the small intestine. **Physical elimination of the remaining residue** generally takes **24–72 hours**.
